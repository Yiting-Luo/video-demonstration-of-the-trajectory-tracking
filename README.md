In summary, across all simulations the decentralized strategy consistently outperforms the centralized benchmark in several respects. In terms of tracking accuracy and disturbance rejection, it achieves smaller transient tracking errors, and under the same process and measurement noises the steady-state position tracking error of the system is about one order of magnitude smaller than that of the centralized benchmark, which indicates its stronger robustness against disturbances. In terms of optimality, its discounted cost converges faster and to a lower value, and the tracking-correction thrust energy of the decentralized strategy is also lower. These advantages stem from the fact that the decentralized strategy exploits the asymmetric information structure, while the centralized benchmark ignores this asymmetry , which degrades its estimation accuracy, increases its cost, and slows its convergence.
